A First-Time Visitor’s Guide to Zanzibar

A practical starting point for planning your first Zanzibar stay, from Stone Town and island rhythm to weather, transport and arrival planning.

Zanzibar rewards travellers who plan the essentials but leave room for the island’s slower rhythm. A good first trip usually combines heritage, coast, food and time to simply settle in.

Start with the geography

Zanzibar is an archipelago off the coast of Tanzania. Unguja and Pemba are its two principal islands, alongside smaller islets. Many first-time visitors begin on Unguja, where Zanzibar City and historic Stone Town are located.

Give Stone Town proper time

Stone Town was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000. UNESCO describes it as an outstanding Swahili trading town whose architecture and urban fabric reflect African, Arab, Indian and European influences built up over centuries.

Rather than treating Stone Town as a quick photo stop, allow time to walk its narrow streets, notice carved doors and courtyards, visit the seafront and understand how living heritage, commerce and tourism share the same compact urban space.

Plan your stay around the kind of trip you want

A beach-focused holiday, a culture-first city stay and a family trip do not need the same base. Before booking, decide whether your priority is walkability, beach access, quiet, nightlife, space for children, a kitchen, or easy transport between different parts of the island.

  • Solo or couple trip: prioritize location, comfort and easy movement.
  • Family stay: look closely at bedroom layout, shared living space and transport needs.
  • Group trip: confirm sleeping arrangements and how the group will move together.
  • Longer stay: kitchen, laundry arrangements, workspace and regular cleaning become more important.

Check current entry requirements before you fly

Travel requirements can change. At the time of publication, official Zanzibar tourism information states that mandatory inbound travel insurance applies to visitors entering Zanzibar. Visa, health and insurance requirements should always be checked against current official government sources shortly before travel.

Build transport into the itinerary

Zanzibar looks compact on a map, but different areas can feel far apart once you account for road conditions, traffic and stops. Pre-arranging airport pickup or key transfers can make the first day much easier, especially for families, groups or late arrivals.

Leave room for more than beaches

The official Zanzibar tourism platforms highlight heritage sites, beaches, forests, spice experiences, marine activities and local events. A balanced itinerary might combine Stone Town, coastal time and one or two experiences rather than trying to move every day.
